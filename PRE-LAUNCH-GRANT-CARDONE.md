# PRE-LAUNCH-GRANT-CARDONE.md
## The Supply Campaign: Strategy, Sales System, and the /agencies Page

This document has three parts. **Part I** explains what we are actually doing and why — read it so every build and copy decision serves the campaign, not just the ticket. **Part II** is the sales system — the doctrine, voice, objections, closing, and follow-up thinking the page embodies. **Part III** is the page itself. The page is one weapon in the campaign; the campaign is the point.

Written in the school of Grant Cardone (Sell or Be Sold; The Closer's Survival Guide) — principles applied, in our words, to our sale.

---

# PART I — THE STRATEGY (what we are doing)

## 1. The objective

The site is built. The bottleneck is now **supply**: profiles. A marketplace with traffic and no inventory converts nothing and convinces no one. The single campaign objective for this phase: **hundreds of real profiles live, fast, concentrated in Amsterdam first** — enough density that (a) real visitors find real choice, (b) every next agency sees a living platform, and (c) momentum is undeniable to anyone evaluating us. Everything in this document optimizes for the speed and volume of consented supply. Cleanup, tiering, and stricter gates come after density exists — that is deliberate sequencing, not a change of values.

## 2. The play

**Agencies, not individuals.** One individual professional = one profile after real convincing. One agency = 5–30 profiles from a single yes. Agencies are the multiplier, so the campaign sells agencies.

**The mechanism: consent-based sync.** The agency gives written permission (the page's form); our crawler imports and continuously synchronizes their roster from their own website. They create no account, upload nothing, maintain nothing. This is the strategic heart: **we removed the entire cost side of their decision.** Nothing is ever scraped before the yes — the consent record is what makes the whole machine clean, and the professionals themselves can always remove their own profiles. That's not compliance overhead; it's a selling point in a market where workers get treated as inventory.

**The frame: pre-launch, not empty.** An empty site begs; a pre-launch site selects. We are assembling a founding cohort of Amsterdam agencies ahead of a public launch. Joining early costs nothing and secures founding position; waiting saves nothing and forfeits it. Every asset — the page, the call, the emails — speaks from inside this frame.

## 3. Why they will say yes (the buyer's math)

The agency owner's world: dependent on one dominant, expensive channel they resent; pitched constantly by new sites that die; allergic to anything that costs staff time. Our offer is engineered against exactly that:

- **The hedge logic (works on skeptics):** they don't need to believe we'll win. A free, zero-effort second channel is rational *even if they think we'll fail* — a costless hedge against their kinky dependency. We never say a word against the incumbent; "a serious alternative finally exists" does its own work.
- **Free leads, direct:** clients contact the agency's own number. No middleman, no cut, no gate. That is the entire value proposition in one sentence.
- **Zero effort, provably:** sync from their own site, review before live, one email to leave. The costs they actually fear — time, lock-in, looking stupid — are each visibly zero.
- **The channel story:** clients increasingly ask AI (ChatGPT) where to go; almost nothing in this industry is built to be the answer; we are. We've run sites in this space and watched AI become a major traffic driver. This is the "why now" and the only place experience gets mentioned.

## 4. Sequence and compounding

1. **Warm before cold.** First yeses come from the warmest relationships — their only job is to make "X and Y are already in" true.
2. **Social proof snowballs.** Every signed agency (that agrees to be named) strengthens the next call and the page. From yes #2 onward the pitch is never "join us," it's "join them."
3. **48-hour delivery is a sales weapon.** Yes on Tuesday, full roster beautifully live for review by Thursday. In a small, talkative industry, speed of follow-through is the story that travels.
4. **Every yes ends with a referral ask.** "Who else should we be talking to?" Warm intros outperform the cold list; the chain is the channel.
5. **One city until dense.** Amsterdam until the founding story is concrete; density where it counts beats coverage everywhere.
6. **After launch: the receipts.** Weekly stats emails to signed agencies (views, contact clicks) are the retention loop and the groundwork for paid visibility later. The free thing must keep proving its value so the paid thing becomes sellable.

## 5. Division of labor

The **caller** (our person in NL) opens and drives; the **page** demonstrates and de-risks; the **form** closes and records consent; the **crawler** delivers the 48-hour wow. Each asset has one job. The page must assume a call happened or is happening — it is the closer's table, not a brochure.

# PART II — THE SALES SYSTEM

## 6. The sale, precisely

- **Reader:** Dutch agency owner, on a phone, mid-call or post-email, professionally skeptical, allergic to effort, tired of being pitched.
- **Conversion:** the consent form submitted. Nothing else counts.
- **Product being sold:** the absence of risk and effort. The platform is the vehicle; the purchase is "nothing to lose."
- **Real objections:** never price (it's free). They are: *time* ("how much work"), *risk* ("will this embarrass or trap us"), *belief* ("you'll be gone in a year"), *inertia* ("we'll see"). Every element of page and pitch must be assignable to killing one of these four. If a sentence doesn't kill one, it's decoration — cut it.

## 7. The doctrine (twelve rules)

1. **Certainty is the product.** A sale is a transfer of conviction. The page states; it never hopes, tries, or aims. Doubt is contagious — so is certainty.
2. **Sell yourself first.** We are sold on this: the product is genuinely better, the offer genuinely free, the risk genuinely zero. Copy written from that conviction reads differently. If a line feels like persuading ourselves, rewrite it or cut the claim.
3. **Always agree.** Never argue with skepticism — voice it first, then answer it. "What's the catch?" is our heading. Objections named by us become trust; objections left to them become exits.
4. **The price myth.** Price is rarely the real objection, and ours is zero — so the true price is time and risk, and the page's job is to make both visibly nothing.
5. **Show, don't claim.** The live demo grid persuades; copy only frames what they're already seeing. Any claim that can be demonstrated must be demonstrated instead of stated.
6. **The small close.** Big decisions get postponed; small ones get made. The ask is a 30-second form and is treated as small: never "application," never "agreement," never ceremony.
7. **Closing is service.** The close benefits *them* — free channel, founding position, zero cost. Hold that frame and persistence stops feeling pushy: not closing them is leaving them worse off. This mindset is what makes asking again natural.
8. **Ask again.** The first no is a reflex, not a decision. Plan for two or three closing attempts per conversation, each from a different angle (hedge → founding window → zero-waste). Most yeses live behind the second ask; almost none behind the sixth.
9. **Stalls are not objections.** "I'll think about it" and "send me something" are stalls — postponements, not refusals. The counter is never information; it's making *now* easier than *later* ("the form takes 30 seconds — I'll stay on the line") and attaching a cost to later (founding window).
10. **The fortune is in the follow-up.** Most yeses arrive on touch 3–6, where competitors have stopped. No lead is dead before five touches; every touch adds something new (a named agency, the live preview promise, a launch-date reminder) — never "just checking in."
11. **Massive action.** Pipeline anxiety, not per-deal anxiety. Enough conversations running that no single no matters — desperation is impossible to hide and fatal to certainty, and the only cure for desperation is volume.
12. **Prosperity posture, zero hype.** Bold ≠ loud. No exclamation marks, no superlatives, no "revolutionary." Cardone-certain is calm, short, declarative. In this industry, loud reads as scam; calm reads as money.

## 8. Voice law (all copy, page and email, now and forever)

- **Shorter and harder always.** Between two versions, ship the shorter, harder one. One idea per sentence. Cut every line in half; ask if it survives another cut.
- Declarative present tense: "Ours is." "You manage nothing." "Free."
- **Banned:** we hope / we try / we aim / we believe / maybe / perhaps / might / "we'd love to" / exclamation marks / emoji / revolutionary / game-changing / cutting-edge / "the #1" / any number we won't show publicly / any competitor's name / any second mention of the team.
- Claims must be one of: demonstrated on the page (the demo), a written commitment (free, 24h removal, review-before-live), or experience as one calm aside (§10.3). Everything else is unclaimable — delete it.
- **Dutch register:** direct je/jij Dutch that hits as hard as the English master. If the Dutch comes out longer and politer, rewrite the Dutch — never pad to sound beleefd. Formal-polite is the wrong voice; direct is trust in this market.
- **The four-question edit test, run on every copy change:** (1) Does it read certain? (2) Can it be shorter? (3) Does it kill a doubt or create one? (4) Would a tired skeptic sneer? Fail any → rewrite. In doubt → delete.

## 9. The objection playbook (agree → answer → close)

Every response has the same skeleton: **agree with the feeling, answer with a fact already on the page, close with a small question.** These are for the caller and for future FAQ/microcopy decisions — the page itself pre-answers most of them.

1. **"We'll wait and see how it goes."** — Fair — that's what everyone smart says about new sites. Here's the thing: waiting doesn't save you anything. It's free and we do all the work — the only thing waiting costs is the founding spot and launch-day presence. *What would you be waiting for that isn't already zero?*
2. **"We're happy with kinky."** — Good — keep it, this isn't instead of anything. It's a second channel that costs nothing and runs itself. Even if we only ever send you a handful of clients, they were free. *Any reason not to take a free hedge?*
3. **"What does it cost later?"** — Straight answer: base listing stays free, permanently. Later there's optional paid visibility if you ever want it — that's our business model, and your profiles are never hostage to it. It's in writing on the page. *Fair enough?*
4. **"Who else is on?"** — [Named founding agencies once true; before that:] You'd be in the first Amsterdam group — that's exactly why the terms are this good. Founding spots are what we're filling this month. *Want one?*
5. **"Who are you? Never heard of you."** — Right, and you shouldn't have — the platform is new. The people behind it aren't; we've run sites in this space for years and built this because the existing ones are stuck in 2012. Open the link — the product answers this better than I can. *Are you near your phone?*
6. **"What about the girls' consent / their data?"** — Best question anyone asks. Everything comes from your own public site, only after your written permission, and any professional can remove her own profile herself, instantly, no questions. We built the whole platform around treating them properly — that's the brand. *Does that cover it?*
7. **"I don't want my site crawled/scraped."** — Understood — and nothing touches your site before your written yes; that's a hard rule of ours. After the yes it's one page-read of your public roster, the same as a visitor opening it. You review everything before it goes live. *Want to see what it produces first? That's exactly what the 48-hour preview is.*
8. **"Send me something by email."** — Already done — it's one link, and it takes less time than this call. The form on it is 30 seconds; I'll stay on the line while you look. *Open it now?*
9. **"I need to think about it."** — Of course. Help me though — it's free, zero work, cancel with one email. Usually "think about it" means one specific doubt I haven't answered. *Which part — the sync, the cost, or us being new?* [Answer that one, then:] *The form's 30 seconds — shall we just do it while we're on?*
10. **"We tried a new site before. It died."** — They mostly do — and you lost the hours you put into it. That's exactly why this one asks for none: if we die, you've lost literally nothing; your profiles just sat on one more site for free. Worst case is zero. *That's the whole design. Fair trade?*

## 10. Close choreography

- **Close on the call.** The link is sent mid-call; the form is completed while the caller stays on the line. Every "later" is a coin-flip lost — the form was engineered to 30 seconds precisely so the yes fits inside the conversation's warmth.
- **Three angles, in order:** the hedge (costs you nothing even if we fail) → the founding window (this is what waiting forfeits) → zero-waste (worst case is zero). One angle per attempt; never repeat an angle back-to-back.
- **After the yes:** confirm the 48-hour preview, set the expectation ("you'll get a link to review — nothing goes live without you"), and immediately ask the referral question: *"Who else should we be talking to?"* The referral ask happens at the moment of maximum goodwill, every time, no exceptions.
- **After a no:** thank, leave the founding window open ("spots are open until [date] — the link works until then"), log the specific objection for follow-up ammunition. A no with a reason is a lead; only silence after five touches is dead.

## 11. Follow-up cadence (the fortune)

- **T0:** call + email with the /agencies link (email exists so the yes has somewhere to land after hours).
- **T+2 days:** short email — one new fact (a named founding agency, or the live preview line: "we can have yours ready to review this week").
- **T+5:** second call. Different angle than the first (per §10).
- **T+9:** "founding window" note — the date is real, the scarcity honest.
- **T+14:** final pre-launch touch. Then park until launch.
- **Post-launch re-approach:** the strongest touch of all — live site, real numbers, named agencies. Every pre-launch no is a launch-week lead.
- Rules: every touch adds something new; "just checking in" is banned; five touches minimum before dead; all touches short and hard per §8.

# PART III — THE /agencies PAGE

The closer's table. Opened from an email or mid-call, on a phone, by a skeptic. Its one conversion: the consent form. It must be the fastest, cleanest page we have — for a pre-launch company, this page's craft is the only claim an agency can verify themselves.

**URL:** `/agencies`. One column, mobile-first, roughly a screen and a half of scrolling on a phone. English master below; NL translation per §8's register.

### §12.1 Hero — declaration, not introduction
> **The new platform for this industry.**
> Launching in Amsterdam. Founding agencies join free.
> [ Join as founding agency ] ← anchors to the form
No welcome, no logo wall, no paragraph. Certainty is brevity.

### §12.2 The demo — the real product
No phone mockups, no screenshots, no device frames. Render the **actual profile grid: 4 profiles of the "Intimate" demo agency**, our real card components exactly as shipped. Tap opens the **real profile modal** — gallery, details, everything. The reader is on a phone; the page itself is the demo.
- Small framing line above: **"This is what your agency looks like on Intimate."**
- Demo profiles flawless, clearly demo-branded.
- **Corridor rule:** no navigation from here into the rest of the site — modal closes back to this page; no header/footer leaks into the (thin) browse experience. Dead-end everything to /agencies.

### §12.3 The shift — why now
> **Clients now ask AI where to go.**
> ChatGPT is rapidly becoming a major driver of clients in this industry — we've run sites in this space and watched it happen. Almost no site is built to be the answer. Ours is.
No numbers, no analytics screenshots, no elaboration. The experience aside appears here once and nowhere else — elaboration invites questions; a calm aside invites belief.

### §12.4 The terms — the founding deal
> - **Free.** The listing costs nothing.
> - **Zero work.** We sync your professionals from your own website. New faces, changes — automatic. You manage nothing.
> - **Direct contact.** Clients reach you on your own number. We take nothing and stand between nothing.
> - **Leave anytime.** One email, everything offline within 24 hours.
Presented as terms of the founding cohort — terms imply a window.

### §12.5 What's the catch? — agree first, then zero-waste
Heading, literally: **"What's the catch?"**
> Profiles are free and stay free. Later we'll offer optional paid visibility — featured placement, if you want it. That's the business. Your profiles are never hostage to it.
Then the risk-reversal close:
> **Worst case: nothing.** Your profiles sit on one more platform. If no clients come, you've spent nothing and lost nothing. If they do, they were free.
Small print, one line: *Pause or remove any profile — or everything — anytime: one email, done within 24 hours. Any professional can also remove her own profile directly.*

### §12.6 How it works — three steps ending in control
> 1. **Say yes** — the form below, 30 seconds.
> 2. **We build it** — your agency page and profiles, synced from your site, within 48 hours.
> 3. **You review it** — nothing goes live until you've seen it.
Step 3 is the quiet closer: they're ordering a preview, not signing up for exposure they can't see.

### §12.7 The form — the consent artifact
Fields: **Agency name · Website URL · Email · Phone (optional).** Nothing else — no password, no account, no "about you."
One checkbox whose label IS the written consent record:
> *I represent this agency and give Intimate permission to import and synchronize the professionals listed on our website. Free of charge; we can withdraw at any time.*
Submit: **"Claim our founding spot"** — never "Submit," never "Apply."
Confirmation: *"Done. Your agency page will be ready for your review within 48 hours. We'll email you the link."*
**The 30-second rule:** completable in under 30 seconds while on the phone with us. It stays this thin forever; any added field is a sale lost.

### §12.8 Sticky mobile CTA
Persistent bottom bar: **"Join as founding agency — free"** → scrolls to the form. The thumb is never more than one tap from the yes.

## 13. What this page must NEVER do

- Show profile counts, traffic stats, or any number we won't stand behind publicly
- Mention investors, backers, funding, or any competitor by name
- Link into the main site's thin browse experience — the demo corridor is the only product surface
- Use device mockups or screenshots where the real component can render
- Carry more than one line about the team, ever
- Ask for anything beyond §12.7's fields
- Grow. New sections require removing sections. Doubt pools in length; the page stays a screen and a half.

## 14. Editing rules (future sessions — every change is a sales decision)

1. Run §8's four-question edit test on every changed line.
2. Friction only goes DOWN: fewer words, fewer fields, fewer taps to the form. Any increase needs explicit justification in the PR description.
3. **Social proof enters only when real:** signed agencies who agree to be named, or a live founding count ("Launching with 12 Amsterdam agencies."). Numbers we stand behind are the only numbers this page ever shows — and they *replace* copy, never extend it. Placement: §12.1 sub-line or §12.4 header.
4. **At launch, retire the founding frame page-wide in one pass** — "founding/pre-launch" on a launched site reads stale, and stale reads failing. Tie the reminder to the launch date.
5. Objection learnings from calls (§9) feed back here: a doubt that keeps surfacing on the phone gets its answer added to the *page* (usually §12.5's small print), because the page must beat the objection before the caller has to.
6. NL translation obeys §8's Dutch register — as short and hard as the English, or the Dutch gets rewritten.
